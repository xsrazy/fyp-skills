# Short Drama Script

Vertical short drama lives or dies on two moments: the first 3 seconds and the last 3 seconds. Everything in the middle exists to connect them. One episode is one conflict, never two.

## Series setup (first request)

Before writing episode 1, lock these with the user or propose them clearly:

- Genre and premise in one sentence. Genres that travel: betrayal, revenge, rich/poor swap, hidden identity, in-law conflict.
- Character cards: name, age, role, one visual anchor (face, hair, outfit color) that never changes, one speech trait.
- The season question: the single mystery or revenge goal that only resolves in the final episode.

Keep the character cards in the conversation and reuse them verbatim in later episodes. If a later request contradicts a card, flag it instead of silently changing the character.

## Episode structure (60-90 seconds, roughly 150-220 spoken words)

1. **Hook (0-3s)**: the most shocking line or image of the episode, shown first. A slap, a reveal, an accusation. Not a greeting, not a setting shot.
2. **Pressure (3-30s)**: the conflict escalates. One character wants something, another blocks it. Every line either attacks, defends, or reveals.
3. **Turn (30-70s)**: new information changes who is winning. A phone is shown, a witness speaks, a lie cracks.
4. **Cliffhanger (last 3-5s)**: stop mid-shock. A door opens, a name is spoken, a sentence is cut in half. Never resolve the episode's conflict and the season question in the same episode.

## Dialogue rules

- Short lines. One breath per line. People under pressure do not give speeches.
- Concrete and conversational. Abstract motivational language reads as fake and kills retention.
- Each character sounds different: vocabulary, sentence length, and what they avoid saying.
- Write for the ear and for subtitles: the key noun or name in each line must be understandable with the sound off, from the subtitle alone.

## Production notes (include with every script)

For each scene, output a compact block the user can feed to image/video tools:

- Scene number and location
- Characters present, with their visual anchors repeated exactly
- Action in one line
- Dialogue lines with speaker names
- One image prompt per scene: vertical 9:16, character anchor, pose, setting, lighting

## Rules

- All story content is original. Do not copy plots, lines, or characters from existing dramas, films, or series.
- Continuity is sacred: names, relationships, who knows what secret, and what happened in earlier episodes must stay consistent. Summarize the story state in 3 lines at the top of each new episode script.
- End every episode script with the cliffhanger line and one line describing what the next episode will open with.
